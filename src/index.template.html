            Sources & References

            The content on this site is organized around the references a radiation oncology team relies on: clinical practice guidelines, standard staging systems, landmark clinical trials, and peer-reviewed literature. This page lists what each module names. It is a map of where the teaching points come from, not a substitute for reading the current versions yourself.

            Reference categories used in the modules

            By disease site

            Guidelines and staging systems are named as they appear in each module. Trials are the ones discussed in that module's readings.

            Primary references for further reading

            Always check the current version of these before applying anything clinically. ASTRO and ACR resources are listed as recommended reading; they are not cited as the basis of specific content on this site.

            • NCCN Clinical Practice Guidelines in OncologyDisease-site treatment guidelines, updated several times a year. Each module's guideline tables are simplified from these for learning.
            • ASTRO Clinical Practice GuidelinesEvidence-based guidelines and statements from the American Society for Radiation Oncology.
            • ACR Appropriateness CriteriaAmerican College of Radiology criteria for imaging and radiation oncology decisions.
            • NRG OncologyThe NCI cooperative group that carries forward the RTOG, NSABP, and GOG trials discussed throughout the modules.

            Educational disclaimer

            Educational use only. This resource is intended for medical education and should not be used as a substitute for clinical judgment, institutional protocols, or current professional guidelines.

            Guideline tables are simplified for learning, doses and regimens vary by institution and protocol, and guidelines change. Clinical cases are teaching scenarios for practice, not recommendations for any individual patient.

            Images

              About

              About RadOnc Sub-I Companion

              RadOnc Sub-I Companion was created as an independent educational resource to make the radiation oncology rotation more structured, approachable, and clinically useful for medical students and junior trainees.

              Why it exists

              Radiation oncology has a steep learning curve. Students are expected to understand disease-site management, staging, imaging, treatment planning, radiation physics, and clinical workflow, often while learning how to present patients in a completely new specialty. That knowledge is usually scattered across guidelines, textbooks, lectures, and conversations in clinic. This site brings it together in the order a sub-intern tends to need it.

              Educational philosophy

              1. 1
                Learn the framework

                Short, structured readings: how the workflow runs, how to present, and how each disease site is staged and treated.

              2. 2
                See it applied

                Interactive anatomy and imaging plates, worked examples, and staging tools put the framework onto real images.

              3. 3
                Practice clinical reasoning

                Step-by-step cases ask you to commit to a stage, a treatment, and a dose before you see the answer.

              4. 4
                Reinforce with spaced repetition

                Flashcards that mirror each reading bring high-yield facts back right before you'd forget them.
